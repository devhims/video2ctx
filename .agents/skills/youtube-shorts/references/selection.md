# Selecting moments

A Short is judged in its first two seconds and finished only if it pays off. Every candidate must be **standalone**: a viewer with no context follows it.

## Find candidates

Collect more than you will keep, roughly two per 10 minutes of talk. Strong sources:

- **The creator's cold open.** Moments the editor already teased before the intro are the creator's own picks.
- **Analogies and images** ("AI agents are like the Minions").
- **Stories** with a setup and a turn ("I walked into the CEO's office unannounced").
- **Contrarian or surprising claims** ("the cyber angle was a distraction").
- **Frameworks and lists** (two measures, three risks) that fit in under a minute.
- **Strong reactions** marked in captions: `[laughter]`, a sudden `>>` exchange, repeated emphasis.
- **Answers to sharp questions.** A short host question followed by the answer is a natural hook and payoff.

Comments and most-replayed data are not available here; the transcript is the evidence.

## Score each candidate

Keep a candidate only when all four hold:

1. **Standalone**: no "as I said", unresolved "he", or reliance on an earlier example. If a reference is unavoidable, add one setup cut or plan the hook text to supply it.
2. **Hook**: the first sentence makes someone stay: a question, a claim, a vivid image, or the start of a story. Throat-clearing ("yeah, so, I think") goes.
3. **Payoff**: it ends on the punchline, conclusion, or turn, never mid-thought.
4. **Length**: 20 to 60 seconds after trimming. Up to 90 seconds only when the story needs it; Shorts allow 180 at most.

Rank by how strong the hook is, then how surprising or useful the payoff is, then how broad its audience is.

## Shape the edit

- **One continuous take** when the moment already flows.
- **Question then answer**: a host's question as cut 1, the answer as cut 2, trimming the host's preamble.
- **Drop the aside**: two cuts around a tangent in the middle.
- **Teaser**: a 2 to 3 second punchline as cut 1, then the full moment. `plan` flags the repeated audio as a warning; that is expected.

Start each cut on the first word of a sentence or speaker turn and end on a sentence end. Keep a speaker's own hedges ("if I understand correctly") when the clip makes a factual claim.

## Risks to note in the brief

- **Unverified claims**: news events, statistics, or accusations. Keep the hedges and keep titles from stating them as fact.
- **Policy-sensitive topics**: weapons, self-harm, dangerous instructions, medical claims, graphic violence. These can limit reach or monetization even as examples.
- **Profanity**: note it so the user can mute or keep it.
- **Third parties**: clips about named people out of context.

## Glossary

Map each misheard phrase to its correction in `glossary`, using the shortest phrase that is unambiguous ("such office" to "Satya's office", not "such" to "Satya"). Matching ignores case and punctuation. Leave anything you cannot confirm out of the glossary and list it as "to check" in the brief.
